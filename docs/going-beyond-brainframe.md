## What is a Personal Knowledge Management System?

A [personal knowledge management
system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_knowledge_management) (PKMS) is
simply a way for you to record what you know in a way that helps you find it
again later. You may have your own method for curating your knowledge, or you
may use someone else's guide to get started. These links are some of the more
popular varieties.

## Methods and Approaches

[Evergreen Notes](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/z5E5QawiXCMbtNtupvxeoEX)
: Designed by Andy Matuschak, this system emphasizes that notes are forever
  being updated. To compare, other systems tend to create notes once and then
  reference them forever without updating them.

[Getting Things Done](https://gettingthingsdone.com/)
: Created by David Allen, this system focuses more on tasks and projects as
  opposed to personal knowledge management. However, by following this system,
  considerable knowledge is built up in written form, so working with a PKMS
  will help you navigate GTD more effectively.

[Linking Your Thinking](https://www.linkingyourthinking.com/)
: Designed by Nick Milo, this system trains you to use Maps of Contents to
  organize your knowledge. This allows you to transcend some of the limitations
  of files and folders to store your data.

[PARA](https://fortelabs.com/blog/para/)
: Designed by Tiago Forte, this system focuses on Projects, Areas, Resources,
  and Archives. This allows you to focus on how much you can do with the ideas,
  starting with your current projects and carrying through to your long term
  archives.

[Zettelkasten](https://zettelkasten.de/introduction/)
: Made famous by Niklas Luhmann, this is a system that was created for a set of
  notecards living in a box. This method provides a set of small notes (usually
  no more than a paragraph or two) that are all interconnected, with the links
  between the notes being the source of inspiration that allowed the creator to
  be as prolific as he was.
